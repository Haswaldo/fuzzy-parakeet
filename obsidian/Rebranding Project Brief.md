# Rebranding Project Brief

**Units:** Unit 9 (Characteristics and Contexts in Media and Communication), Unit 10 (Engaging with an Audience in Creative Media Production). Feeds into Unit 13, Extended Project in Creative Media Production.

## Aim
Rebrand an existing company for a chosen target audience: research why rebrands fail, then build a complete brand kit, touchpoints and mock-ups through planned rounds of revision. Show that a brand is developed through testing, feedback and revision, not in one attempt.

## Task 1: Context  `U10 1.1 / U9 1.1`
Analyse the characteristics of different audiences and contexts in branding.

1. **Branding vs rebranding**: one-page explainer (infographic or annotated mind map). Include:
   - Branding = building identity, recognition and trust from scratch.
   - Rebranding = changing an identity audiences already recognise.
   - Refresh (evolution, e.g. updated logo) vs full rebrand (revolution: new name, look, positioning).
   - Why companies rebrand: new audience, dated image, merger, reputation problem, change in business.
   - Why rebranding is riskier: what an existing audience stands to lose.
   - One real example each: launched from scratch, refresh, full rebrand.
2. **Branding timeline** in Canva: origins to today. Milestones, dates, designers, agencies, examples of identity change.
3. **Animated presentation: anatomy of a brand** (name, logo, colour, typography, imagery, tone of voice, values, positioning). At least **5 companies**, with video sources, and the target audience each element is designed for.
4. **3 companies for "brand evolution" research**: who are they, what do they do, how has the identity changed to keep or win the audience?

**Evidence:** explainer, timeline (and MP4 if completed), Prezi (anatomy of a brand), audience analysis of 3 companies.

## Task 2: Research, failures and target audience  `U10 1.2 / U9 1.2`
Build a **research portfolio in Canva**.

1. **3+ failed rebrands.** For each: what changed, why, audience response, cost, recovery. Starting points: Gap (2010), Tropicana (2009), Royal Mail / Consignia (2001), Jaguar (2024), Cracker Barrel (2025).
2. **3+ successful rebrands.** e.g. Burberry (2023), Mastercard (2016), Airbnb (2014). What did they keep, test or explain that the failures didn't?
3. **Brand equity**: which assets audiences value most (logo, colour, packaging shape, characters, sonic branding) and why removing them is risky.
4. **Audience targeting**: segmentation, personas, audience research.
5. **Testing**: focus groups, A/B testing, social listening, soft launches.
6. **Backlash**: how social media and algorithms amplify reaction, how it spreads, how companies respond.
7. **Target audience**: one specific demographic for each of the 3 failed case studies to rebrand for. **This audience stays the same for the rest of the brief.** Profile: age, location, lifestyle, income; media habits; values; brands they love and why; a persona.

**Evidence:** research portfolio (Canva), 3 failure case studies, 3 success comparisons, audience profile and persona, primary audience research findings.

## Task 3: Problem solving, brand audit  `U9 1.2 / U10 1.2, 2.1`
Pick **3 companies** whose branding fails to reach your Task 2 audience (local or national). Audit each with **SWOT**, using the lessons from Task 2, then choose one to rebrand.

**Rebrand rationale** for the chosen company:
- **The problem**: why current branding doesn't reach your audience, with evidence.
- **The audience**: why they are the right audience for this business.
- **Brand equity**: what to keep, what to change.
- **The risk**: which Task 2 failure you are most at risk of repeating, and how you'll avoid it.

### Class activity: The Pitch
You are a design agency pitching for the rebrand contract.
- **Lesson 1, build the pitch deck (max 6 slides):**
  1. The company and its current branding
  2. The problem, with evidence
  3. Your target audience and why they matter to the business
  4. The big idea, in one sentence
  5. What you'll keep and what you'll change
  6. The risk, and how you'll avoid it
- **Lesson 2, deliver:** 5 minutes pitching + 3 minutes questions. Record the panel's feedback and use it to strengthen the rationale.

**Evidence:** SWOT audits of 3 companies, rebrand rationale for the chosen company.

## Task 4: Planning and production  `U9 2.1, 2.2 / U10 2.2`

### Planning: development and revisions (digital sketchbook)
1. Initial concepts: mood boards and **3+ distinct creative routes**.
2. **3+ revision rounds** (v1, v2, v3) on the chosen route. Per round: before/after side by side, feedback received, what changed and why.
3. Rejected routes and the reason for each decision.
4. Version control: label every version (e.g. `Brand_Logo_v1`, `Brand_Logo_v2`).

### Production: brand kit (Adobe InDesign)
1. Brand story, values, positioning
2. Logo suite: primary, secondary, icon, mono/reversed
3. Logo usage: clear space, minimum size, don'ts
4. Colour palette with HEX, RGB and CMYK
5. Typography and type hierarchy
6. Imagery, iconography, graphic devices
7. Tone of voice, with examples

### Production: touchpoints
1. **Touchpoint map**: persona's customer journey from discovery to purchase, touchpoints marked at each stage.
2. **Touchpoint designs**: flat artwork across print and digital, whole business (shopfront/signage, website/app, social, stationery, uniform, packaging, out-of-home, merchandise, vehicle livery).
3. **Rationale**: annotate each touchpoint for why it suits the audience and how it follows the brand kit.

### Production: mock-ups (Photoshop)
Smart-object mock-ups (shopfront, phone, billboard, uniform, van). Must cover **every stage of the journey**, show **print and digital**, and look like **one consistent brand** side by side.

**Evidence:** development log (mood boards, 3 routes, v1-v3, rejected routes), brand kit (InDesign PDF), touchpoint map and designs, Photoshop mock-ups.

## Task 5: Evaluation  `U9 3.1, 3.2 / U10 3.1, 3.2`
Written evaluation in the digital sketchbook covering:
- Success of research, idea development, final outcome
- What worked, what didn't, how you worked
- How revision rounds changed the final identity, and which feedback mattered most
- How well the rebrand appeals to the target audience, and how it holds up against the Task 2 failures
- What you'd do differently

Get feedback from **members of your target audience** as well as peers.

**Evidence:** written evaluation, target audience and peer feedback.

## Task 6: Presentation, brand launch  `U10 2.2`
Present the brand kit, touchpoints and mock-ups to a client panel. Create a **digital platform** for the rebranded company with **at least 3 different ways** for the target audience to engage.

**Evidence:** brand launch presentation, link to digital platform.

## Reference: "What is a rebrand?" explainer (attached image)
Branding Basics, Creative Media & Marketing.

- **A rebrand** is when a company changes how it looks, sounds or presents itself, to change what people think and feel about it.
- **What can change:** name, logo, colours, typography, slogan, tone of voice, packaging, audience.
- **Why rebrand:** looking old-fashioned; reaching a new audience; doing new things; a merger or new owner; fixing a bad reputation; standing out.
- **How big a change:** brand refresh (tidy logo, colours, fonts) / partial rebrand (new look and message, often for a new audience) / full rebrand (everything can change, even the name).
- **Identity vs image:** identity = what you *see* (logo, colours, fonts); image = what you *think and feel* about it.
- **The process:** 1 Research (audit brand, audience, rivals), 2 Strategy (values, positioning, audience), 3 Design (new identity and guidelines), 4 Launch (reveal with a campaign), 5 Evaluate (did views change?).
- **The risks:** loyal customers may dislike it; expensive; people can get confused. Case in point: Gap launched a new logo in 2010 and scrapped it within a week after online complaints.
- **Real-world rebrands:** Twitter to X (2023), Dunkin' Donuts to Dunkin' (2019), Old Spice to New image (2010), Burberry to Heritage look (2023).
- **Your turn:** pick a brand you know. If you rebranded it: why does it need a rebrand? Who is the new target audience? Refresh, partial or full? What would you change, and keep?
